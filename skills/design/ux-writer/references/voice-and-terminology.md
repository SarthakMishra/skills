# Establish product voice and terminology

Read this file when the task establishes product voice, adapts tone, names a
concept, or resolves inconsistent vocabulary. Start with the project's guidance
and audience evidence. Voice is the stable set of writing choices across a
product. Tone changes with the situation.

## Find the basis for the voice

1. Read the brief, product principles, audience research, style guide, and
   approved interface examples. Keep explicit requirements ahead of preferences
   inferred from a single screen.
2. Identify who uses the interaction, what they want, and what relationship the
   product should establish. A buyer's priorities may differ from a user's.
3. Separate stable writing choices from tone tied to one state. A campaign slogan
   or celebratory screen is weak evidence for the whole interface.
4. If evidence conflicts, name the conflict and propose a treatment for this task.
   If evidence is absent, use a neutral provisional voice. Do not invent brand
   values or require a branding exercise for a small copy edit.

Done means the source, audience, stable choices, situation, conflicts, and
provisional assumptions are explicit.

Bad: infer the whole product voice from one cheerful onboarding screen.

Good: use the style guide and approved error messages for stable choices, then
adapt warmth for onboarding and restraint for failure.

When a product voice definition is requested, propose missing choices and identify
which ones need a product decision. Keep proposals separate from adopted policy.

## Turn principles into writing choices

For recurring work or an explicit voice assignment, create a compact voice chart.
Use product principles as columns and fill only the relevant cells.

| Aspect         | Specify                                                                                                   |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Concepts       | Ideas to emphasize when they help the task and associations to avoid.                                     |
| Vocabulary     | Words that express the product's relationship with its audience. Keep domain definitions in the glossary. |
| Verbosity      | Where terse labels suffice and where explanation or reassurance is needed.                                |
| Grammar        | Point of view, contractions, sentence structure, and formality.                                           |
| Punctuation    | Treatment of questions, emphasis, pauses, and fragments.                                                  |
| Capitalization | Case for headings, controls, names, and status text.                                                      |

`Friendly` alone cannot settle a writing decision.

Bad: `Use a friendly tone.` It gives two writers no shared choice to apply.

Good: `Use direct address in setup instructions. Acknowledge disruption before
explaining recovery.`

Use the principle most relevant to the moment. When principles conflict, preserve
comprehension and consequence, then record the specific voice tradeoff. Do not
force every principle into every string.

Done means another writer can apply the chart to the same situation and produce
the same kind of choice.

## Adapt tone to the situation

| Situation                       | Treatment                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unfamiliar task                 | Explain the immediate value and next decision; match the audience's expertise.                       |
| Frequent routine action         | Use compact, predictable wording. Let the completed action provide closure.                          |
| Meaningful achievement          | Celebrate when the voice and the person's likely experience support it.                              |
| Failure or disruption           | Acknowledge the actual problem and help the person recover. Avoid blame and unsupported reassurance. |
| Money, access, consent, or loss | Make the consequence explicit. Keep personality subordinate to the facts.                            |

The same completed action can support different tones. A formal training product
can say `Lesson completed.` A playful learning product can say `You finished the
lesson!` Neither voice licenses `You mastered this` unless mastery was assessed.

Sentence case and ordinary punctuation can fill gaps, but they do not override an
established usable convention. Contractions, first-person labels, apologies,
exclamation marks, and humor are contextual choices. Keep the facts and recovery
first in failures and consequential choices.

Done means the chosen tone fits the state, preserves meaning, and does not turn an
unsupported claim into personality.

## Keep terminology stable

Read the project's glossary, domain documentation, existing interface, research,
search terms, and support language before renaming anything.

- Give each concept one preferred term. Document distinct meanings when similar
  terms must coexist, such as a saved draft and a published version.
- Preserve precise professional language for expert tasks. Explain unfamiliar
  terms at first use for other audiences instead of replacing precision with
  vagueness.
- Name ordinary objects recognizably. Coin a term only for a distinct concept
  whose benefit justifies learning it, and define it where needed.
- Treat related options as a set. People must distinguish them without opening
  every destination or remembering another screen.

For a terminology review, record the concept, preferred term, definition, existing
variants, and affected locations. Propose migrations explicitly. Shared string
keys may serve different contexts, so inspect their uses before replacing them.

For localized products, keep the concept stable while allowing translators to
choose natural terms and grammar. Preserve a literal explanation of deliberate
wordplay. Translating English wording does not prove that the same relationship or
meaning survives.

Done means each changed concept has a preferred term, definition, affected
locations, and a migration or exception decision.
